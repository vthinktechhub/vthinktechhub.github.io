Sponsor a Project

Why Sponsor a Project?

Sponsoring a project lets your company put a real industry problem in front of motivated innovators, first-generation founders and entrepreneurs, while moving long-running problems or early-stage ideas forward. Teams are mentored throughout the project and deliver a working prototype with clear documentation.

What Should Sponsors Expect?

  • You get to know innovators who chose your problem, which can lead to hiring or a long-term partnership.
  • Advising the team and receiving project status updates, about one hour a week.
  • You advance your problem without using in-house resources.
  • Your company becomes known within the grassroots founder community.
  • You help first-time founders learn by building real solutions.

What a Sponsor Gets Back

01

Move a stalled problem forward

Advance a long-running problem or an early idea without pulling your own team off their work.

02

Meet people worth hiring

Get to know innovators who chose your problem. Sponsors often hire the people who solved theirs.

03

A working prototype

Teams are mentored to the finish and hand over a functional prototype with clear documentation.

04

Back the grassroots

Help first-generation, first-time founders learn by shipping real work, with your name behind it.

Sponsor Requirements

Advisory Role

Name one advisor, mentor or POC from your company. Budget about an hour a week to guide the team and answer questions.

Resources

No fixed fee. Share what you can: data, equipment, funding or access to your systems. We scope the project with you.

Intellectual Property

Ownership of the work and how your company is credited are agreed in writing before the project begins.

Deadlines

Projects run in cohorts. Send your proposal before the next intake. If the timing doesn't suit you, contact us.

How It Works

Startup track

Ideas become startups

Founders bring their own idea and the hub helps turn it into a company.

Sponsored projects

Industry problems become projects

A company brings a real problem and resources, and a hub team takes it on as a project with the sponsor's mentor.

From Problem Statement to Reviewed Result

  1. Industry posts a use case

    A company submits a real problem statement and the resources it can offer.

  2. The hub matches a team

    We scope the brief with the sponsor and match it to innovators whose skills fit.

  3. The team builds

    The team works on it as a project, guided by the sponsor's advisor or POC.

  4. The sponsor reviews

    Results are presented at Demo Day, and the sponsor decides what to adopt or extend.

Who the Hub Is For

Innovators

People who have spotted a problem in their community, workplace or field and want to fix it properly.

First-generation founders

The first in their family to start a business, who need mentors, networks and real customers early.

First-time entrepreneurs

People new to startups who want to learn by shipping real work for a real sponsor.
